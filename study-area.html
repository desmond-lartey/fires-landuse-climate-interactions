Study area

15 protected areas across four West African ecological zones

The study region spans Upper Guinea, 5°N–10°N and 10°W–5°E, across Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana, Togo, Burkina Faso, Benin, and Nigeria. Annual rainfall in the region peaks near 4,000 mm/yr and declines to about 1,200 mm/yr at the forest–savanna boundary, falling further in the driest segments.

Regional context

Parks were selected to represent a gradient from continuous Sudanian savanna in the north to closed-canopy moist forest in the south, with forest–savanna mosaics and montane transition systems in between. This spread is what makes it possible to separate the effect of ecological zone from park-specific management or boundary pressure.

How the four ecological zones were defined

Zones are a regional classification, not a global biome scheme. They combine three criteria:

  • Mean annual rainfall, following Tano et al. (2023)
  • Dry season length, following Eva & Lambin (2000) and Ouattara et al. (2024)
  • Vegetation structure, following Ametsitsi et al. (2020), Janssen et al. (2018), Liu et al. (2016)
Northern Savanna

Sudanian savanna

Open canopy, continuous grass cover, long dry season. Frequent, extensive, low-intensity fire is expected.

Northern Forest

Woodland–forest mosaic

More humid than Northern Savanna. Fire expected to be less frequent and more spatially patchy.

Southern Transition

Forest–savanna mosaic

Tree and grass dominance co-exist under moderate rainfall. Intermediate, interannually variable fire activity.

Southern Forest

Humid tropical forest

Closed canopy, high rainfall, limited fire spread. Rare fire, but locally severe under drought.

These zone–fire associations are treated as hypotheses tested against the 15 study parks, not assumptions, see Results for how each zone actually behaved.

Park boundary asset

All zonal statistics are computed against one merged Earth Engine FeatureCollection of the 15 park boundaries, reduced to a single identifying field:

boundary referencejs
var parks = ee.FeatureCollection('projects/ee-desmond/assets/NewParkMerged')
  .select(['ORIG_NAME']);

Every downstream script in GEE scripts starts from this same asset, so buffer geometries, land-cover histograms, and climate means are always keyed to the same ORIG_NAME values used in the tables above.
