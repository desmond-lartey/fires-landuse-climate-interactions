Manuscript in production · TRC-26-0053

Fire, land use, and climate interactions in West African conservation landscapes

A spatially explicit, buffer-based analysis of 20 years (2004–2024) of MODIS burned-area detections across 15 protected areas in Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana, Togo, Burkina Faso, Benin, and Nigeria — integrating harmonized GLAD/ESRI land cover and ERA5-Land climate reanalysis to explain why fire regimes differ across ecological zones, buffer distance, and vegetation type.

Period 2004–2024 Protected areas 15 Ecological zones 4 Buffer distances 0, 5, 10, 15, 20 km Countries CI · GH · TG · BF · BJ · NG

Data inputs

Four remotely sensed layers

MODIS MCD64A1 burned area (500 m), harmonized GLAD 2000–2020 / ESRI 2021–2024 land cover (30 m / 10 m), and ERA5-Land monthly climate reanalysis (~25 km), reduced to park- and buffer-level zonal statistics in Google Earth Engine.

Spatial framework

Interior-to-edge gradients

Each park is analysed as five concentric units — the interior (0 km) and buffer rings at 5, 10, 15, and 20 km — so that fire exposure, land cover, and climate can be compared from park core to surrounding human-modified landscape.

Research questions

The analysis was structured around five questions that move from park-level description toward the spatial and temporal mechanisms that explain observed differences:

  1. Why do protected areas differ in frequency of burning?
  2. How is overall annual burning related to rainfall and temperature variability, particularly during dry years?
  3. What is the relationship between fire activity, location, climate, and land cover types?
  4. Do different land cover types burn at different frequencies, extents, and seasonal timings?
  5. How does the timing of fires vary between inside and outside of protected areas?

Five-step analytical workflow

Every output on this site traces back to one of five processing steps, all implemented in Google Earth Engine and chained through a single set of park, buffer, year, and month keys. Full code is on the GEE scripts page; parameter-by-parameter documentation is on the API reference page.

Step 1

Burned-area harmonization

MODIS MCD64A1 monthly composites, 2004–2024, summed per calendar month into a 20-year climatology.

Step 2

Buffer disaggregation

Zonal reduction of burned area across five concentric analysis units per park (0–20 km).

Step 3

Land-cover harmonisation

GLAD (2000–2020) and ESRI (2021–2024) reclassified to one 9-class scheme, sampled at benchmark years.

Step 4

Climate integration

ERA5-Land monthly reanalysis reduced to zonal means per park, buffer, and year.

Step 5

Seasonal fire–rainfall coupling

Dry- vs wet-season OLS regressions of burned area against precipitation, plus a lagged wet-season fuel-load test.

Ecological zones used in this study

Zones are region-specific groupings based on mean annual rainfall, dry-season length, and vegetation structure — not global biome classes. They are the primary explanatory grouping used throughout the results.
