Methodology

A five-step workflow from raw satellite composites to a park-buffer-year dataset

Each step below states its exact inputs, the Earth Engine operations applied, its output units, and where it feeds into the next step. The corresponding code lives on the GEE scripts page and is cross-referenced throughout.

Reconciling resolutions

Burned area (MODIS, 500 m), land cover (GLAD/ESRI, 30 m), and climate (ERA5-Land, ~25 km) are never resampled onto a common pixel grid. Instead, each layer is reduced independently to zonal statistics within the same park/buffer geometry — burned-area sums at 500 m, land-cover class fractions at 30 m, climate means at ERA5's native ~11–25 km — and the resulting scalars are joined by (park, buffer, year). This avoids introducing resampling artefacts while still allowing direct comparison across layers.

Step 1

Burned-area harmonization (2004–2024)

Input: MODIS MCD64A1 Collection 6.1, filtered to the study extent. Output: 12 monthly composite images, each summing 21 years of fire detections for that calendar month.

  1. For each year 2004–2024 and each calendar month, filter MCD64A1 to that one-month window and mosaic the collection over the study area.
  2. Select the BurnDate band and threshold it (BurnDate > 0), producing a binary "did this pixel burn in this month/year" mask.
  3. Stack the 21 yearly binary masks for a given calendar month into an ImageCollection and take the pixel-wise sum(). The result is an integer image: for each pixel, "in how many of the 21 years did this month record a burn?"
  4. Repeat for all 12 months, producing SummedBurnedArea_Month1 … _Month12 (one asset per calendar month), each exported at 500 m as a GeoTIFF and, separately, to an Earth Engine asset.

The downstream fire-regime analysis re-uses six of these — October through March, the dry-season window — as SummedBurnedArea_Month10 … Month3.

Why a 20-year composite, not per-year Pixel-level fire behaviour showed no large year-to-year discontinuity across the study period, so a single 20-year monthly composite (rather than 20 separate annual layers) was used as the foundation for spatial disaggregation, keeping the buffer-zone reduction in Step 2 computationally tractable across 15 parks × 5 buffers × 6 months.

Step 2

Buffer-based disaggregation of fire frequency and burned area

Input: the 15-park boundary FeatureCollection and the six monthly composite images from Step 1. Output: one feature per (park × buffer distance), carrying monthly burn frequency, monthly burned area (km²), and two normalised burn-exposure metrics.

  1. For each park, generate five analysis units: the interior boundary (0 km) and buffers at 5, 10, 15, and 20 km, built with geometry().buffer(distance).
  2. For each analysis unit and each of the six dry-season months, run reduceRegion with ee.Reducer.sum() at scale = 500 m against the corresponding monthly composite, reading the BurnDate band — this gives burn frequency (pixel-years) for that month.
  3. Convert frequency to area: burned_km² = frequency × 500 × 500 / 1e6.
  4. Compute buffer area (geometry().area() / 1e6), the implied pixel count at 500 m resolution, and two summary rates:
    • AvgBurnPerPixel = Σ(monthly frequencies) / TotalPixels
    • AvgBurnPerYear = AvgBurnPerPixel / 22 (climatology length)

This produces a fire-exposure gradient — highest at 0 km, generally declining toward 20 km — that is comparable across parks and ecological zones regardless of park size.

Step 3

Land-cover harmonisation and annual composition

Input: GLAD GLCLU2020 v2 annual layers (2000–2020, 30 m) and the ESRI Global 10 m Annual LULC time series (2017–2024). Output: one 9-class land-cover image per benchmark year, and per-buffer class-frequency histograms.

  1. Following the harmonisation approach of Xu et al. (2024), remap GLAD's native class codes (0–211, including disturbance and height-gain sub-codes) into the 9-class scheme with an explicit ee.Dictionary lookup — see gladToEsri on the GEE scripts page for the full mapping.
  2. Remap ESRI's native 11-class codes (1,2,4,5,7,8,9,10,11) onto the same 9-class scheme with image.remap(...).
  3. Resolve a "which dataset for which year" function: GLAD for 2000 ≤ year ≤ 2020, ESRI for year ≥ 2021.
  4. For each protected area × buffer × benchmark year (2000, 2005, 2010, 2015, 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024), run reduceRegion with ee.Reducer.frequencyHistogram() at scale = 30 m, returning the pixel count for each of the 9 classes within that analysis unit.

Resolution reconciliationLand-cover fractions are computed at native 30 m resolution and then aggregated to the park/buffer level — burned-area pixels (500 m) are never resampled down to 30 m, and land-cover pixels are never resampled up to 500 m. The two layers are joined only after both have already been reduced to zonal summaries for the same geometry.

Step 4

Integration of climatic drivers

Input: ECMWF/ERA5_LAND/MONTHLY_AGGR. Output: eight climate variables per park × buffer × year, in physical units.

  1. For a given year, filter ERA5-Land to Jan 1 – Dec 31 and take the temporal mean of eight bands: 2 m mean/min/max temperature, 2 m dewpoint temperature, total precipitation sum, surface pressure, and the 10 m U/V wind components.
  2. Convert units: temperatures K → °C (subtract 273.15); precipitation m → mm (×1000); pressure Pa → hPa (÷100). Wind components are left in m/s.
  3. Run reduceRegion with ee.Reducer.mean() at scale = 11,132 m (ERA5-Land's native grid), weighted so cropped grid cells at park/buffer edges are handled as area-weighted means.

These eight zonal means are attached to every park-buffer-year feature as ERA5_Tmean_C, ERA5_Tmin_C, ERA5_Tmax_C, ERA5_Tdew_C, ERA5_Precip_mm, ERA5_SurfacePressure_hPa, ERA5_WindU, and ERA5_WindV.

Step 5

Seasonal fire–rainfall coupling (dry vs. wet)

Input: MCD64A1 monthly composites and ERA5-Land monthly aggregates, 2004–2024. Output: park-level OLS regression statistics at two temporal scales, plus a lagged wet-season fuel-load test.

  1. Define seasons following Ichoku et al. (2016): dry season = October–March, wet season = April–September.
  2. Sum MCD64A1 monthly burned area (pixel-area weighted) within each season/year/park to get seasonal burned-area totals; sum ERA5-Land monthly precipitation the same way for seasonal rainfall totals. Both are computed with zonal reductions in Earth Engine.
  3. Within-year regression (2024 only): regress monthly burned area against monthly precipitation using all buffer-zone observations (0–20 km) as data points — one OLS fit per park.
  4. Multi-year regression (2004–2024): regress total burned area against annual precipitation using all buffer-zone × year observations per park (5 buffers × ~20 years ≈ 100 points per park), giving enough variance for slope estimation.
  5. Pooled regression: combine all parks and years into one OLS fit to test the overall precipitation–burned-area relationship.
  6. Lagged fuel-load test (following Archibald et al., 2009): regress dry-season burned area against the preceding wet-season rainfall (April–September of year t−1) to test whether fuel accumulation, rather than concurrent moisture, better predicts dry-season burning.

Known limitations

Satellite-derived burned-area products may under-detect understory fires beneath closed canopy. Socio-economic drivers of ignition (e.g. agricultural burning calendars, pastoralist movement) were not spatially mapped and are inferred from timing patterns only. Repeated buffer-zone observations within a park are not statistically independent, which is why pooled regression R² is modest even where the effect direction is consistent.
